0. Set the target number of labs in config.py
1. Run `python3 prepare_data.py`. Before that, you can also look into data.json and remove recipes you don't like by adjusting REMOVE_RECIPE_GROUPS or REMOVE_RECIPES variables in prepare_data.py. You can also adjust AWKWARD_CONTRACT_PRODUCTS to remove/add some contracts.
2. Open calculator.py and adjust LOGISTICS_COST, SIZE_COST, and RAW_RESOURCE_COSTS variables. LOGISTICS_COST penalizes total i/o in your factory, i.e. trucks and belts. SIZE_COST penalizes your factory's footprint, i.e. total building area. RAW_RESOURCE_COSTS are costs for mining each raw resource. You can balance these costs, or set some of them them to ~zero and prioritize logistics, footprint, or mining.
3. Look at the main() function inside calculator.py. Here you can see various global settings for your factory, like the settlement level (defined by the settlement's unity multiplier), edict options, and choices of services to provide to your settlement. You can try to optimize over all possible combinations, or reduce the search space by only leaving options you know you want. IMPORTANT NOTE: if you want to try a wide range of combinations, set startup_trials in main() to 500-1000 (and maybe increase early_stopping_rounds). The search may take a while.
4. Run `python3 calculator.py` and look into the out/ folder. Here you can see various files describing the optimal factory the optimizer found. factory_summary_best.json shows how much of each item is used in the factory and the recipes that consume/provide it. recipes_summary_best.json shows all the used recipes, their flow (which represents how many machines are required to run the recipe), the per-recipe cost, and the recipe cost scaled by the flow. shadow_prices_best.json shows the marginal cost/value of each item (negative for byproducts), and reduced_costs_best.json shows how far are the alternative recipes from being used in the optimal solution (zero for used recipes).
